How it works

Three steps, about the time it takes to hesitate.

You decide what gets checked and when. There is no always-on monitoring.

  1. 1

    Choose what to check

    Open ScamLens on the page you’re viewing, select text and use the right-click menu, or paste a link or message into the box. A suspicious email, text or chat reply works the same way.

  2. 2

    We look for warning signs

    The visible text and links are analysed for the patterns scams rely on, such as urgency, requests for payment or codes, and links that hide their destination. The URL is also checked for structural red flags.

  3. 3

    Read the report, then act

    You get a clear result with the exact quotes that triggered it, factual notes about the domain, and numbered next steps. Copy the report to share with someone you trust.

What it checks

The patterns behind most scams.

Each finding quotes the exact words from the page, so you can judge it yourself.

Pressure to act quickly

Tight deadlines, “final warning” language and threats of suspension are designed to stop you from thinking.

Requests for payment details

Asking for card numbers, fees or gift cards through a link you didn’t start.

Secret codes and passwords

One-time passcodes, PINs and logins are things a real service never asks you to hand over like this.

Discouraging verification

“Don’t contact anyone” or “keep this confidential” makes independent checking harder, which is the point.

Unexpected prizes and refunds

Rewards you never signed up for are a common way to start a conversation that ends with a payment.

Link and domain observations

URL shorteners, look-alike characters, embedded usernames, raw IP addresses and missing HTTPS, reported as facts rather than accusations.

The report

An answer you can check, not a black box.

Every result is built to be read in under a minute and to leave the decision with you.

  • A plain-language result. Likely scam, High risk, Medium risk, Low risk, No threat found, or Unclear.
  • Evidence you can verify. The exact text that raised each flag, with a short explanation.
  • Domain notes. Whether the connection is encrypted, and a clear statement of what that does and doesn’t prove.
  • What to do next. Numbered steps, such as opening the official app yourself instead of using the link.
  • Feedback. Think a result is wrong? Report it in one click.

Possible results

  • Likely scamSeveral strong warning signs.
  • High riskTreat as dangerous until verified.
  • Medium riskWarning signs that warrant caution.
  • Low riskOnly limited warning signs.
  • No threat foundNothing suspicious in what was checked.
  • UnclearNot enough information for a verdict.

“No threat found” is never a guarantee of safety, and “Unclear” never means safe or dangerous on its own.

Privacy

You control when anything is read or sent.

ScamLens is deliberately plain about what it handles.

What is sent, and when

Only when you press Analyze or use the right-click menu, the extension sends the page address, title, visible text and up to 60 links (or the text you selected or pasted) over HTTPS to the ScamLens analysis service to produce your report.

If you sign in, your request also carries a sign-in token so the service knows whose history to keep.

What never happens

  • No scanning in the background or on pages you didn’t choose.
  • No reading of other tabs or your browsing history.
  • No ads, and no selling of what you scan.
  • No passwords or card numbers are ever requested by the extension.

Your history, your choice

Signed out, your past scans are saved only on your device. Signed in with Google, they’re kept in your ScamLens account. Either way you can delete one item or clear everything from the History page.

Sign-in is optional

You can scan without an account. Signing in with Google only adds account-based history; it doesn’t change what the result says.

Good to know

What ScamLens can’t do.

A good tool is clear about its limits. These matter.

It’s an assessment, not proof. A flagged page isn’t a confirmed scam, and a clean result isn’t a guarantee of safety. Scammers adapt, and some pages are genuinely ambiguous.

It’s one input, not the final word. When money, accounts or personal details are involved, confirm through a contact you find yourself, such as the company’s official app or a number printed on your card.

Unknown isn’t the same as safe. If a site’s reputation can’t be checked, ScamLens says so rather than guessing.

FAQ

Common questions.

Does ScamLens scan every page I visit?

No. It reads a page only when you open the extension and choose to analyze it, use the right-click menu, or paste something in. It does nothing in the background.

Which browsers does it work with?

Chrome and Microsoft Edge, as well as other Chromium-based browsers that support Manifest V3 extensions.

Can I check a text message or email that isn’t on a web page?

Yes. Paste the message or a link into the box in the extension, or copy it into any page and select it. ScamLens checks the wording and any links it contains.

Do I need an account?

No. Signing in with Google is optional and only adds history that’s stored in your account instead of on your device.

What if the result is wrong?

Use Report incorrect result on the report. Feedback helps improve future results.

If it says “No threat found”, can I relax?

Treat it as a good sign, not a guarantee. If something asks for money, codes or passwords, verify it independently regardless of the result.
